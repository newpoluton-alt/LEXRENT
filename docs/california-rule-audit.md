## AB 325 effective-date evidence

D022 is the chaptered AB 325 text, Chapter **338**, approved October 6, 2025. It contains neither an urgency provision nor a different activation date. January 1, 2026 follows ordinary statutory operation under Article IV §8(c), independently corroborated by the [California Senate legislative calendar](https://www.senate.ca.gov/legislative-deadlines), whose 2026 section lists “Jan 01, 2026” and “Statutes take effect (Art. IV, Sec. 8(c)).” The authentic calendar is now captured as **S017**, [`corpus/sources/S017.txt`](../corpus/sources/S017.txt), with retrieval method `web_reader_html_capture`. It is a general statutory calendar, not an AB-325-specific effective-date announcement: the date is an explicit inference from S017 together with D022's non-urgency chaptered enactment. The rule's `supporting_source_doc_ids` and `effective_date_basis` record that distinction. Fixture T1 was not used as legal evidence or as the source of the date.

The [Judicial Council 2025 legislative summary](https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/2025-summary-final.pdf) has an indexed AB 325 entry with an explicit January 1, 2026 date. Its chapter number is 328, inconsistent with chaptered D022 and the [Assembly Judiciary final summary](https://ajud.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2025-10/2025-asm-jud-bill-summary.pdf), both Chapter 338. The chaptered text governs the citation. Direct retrieval of the Judicial Council PDF returned 403 during this review; an indexed excerpt must be identified as an excerpt, not a full PDF capture. A [CalPERS January 2026 presentation](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/documents/202601-full-what-we-need-to-know-powerpoint-a/download?inline=) also has an indexed AB 325 / January 1, 2026 row, but direct retrieval likewise returned 403.

Only AB 325 is represented in `CA-ALG-01`. The fixture’s mention of SB 763 does not authorize inventing SB 763 operative text or enforcement penalties absent source capture.

## Executable fact contract

Missing boolean or ownership facts stay missing. They must not be defaulted to false. All coverage flags are reviewed determinations with supporting records, not guesses from a postal city or building age.

- **Municipality:** city rules require a verified legal municipality. The San Ysidro postal alias is not a separate government. A wrong verified city must not leak another city’s rules.
- **Certificate:** use `certificate_of_occupancy_date` and the engine’s derived age on the requested date. A construction year does not supply a certificate date. Conventional apartment coverage is the tested scope; mobilehome-specific statutory exceptions are not modeled as if these parcels were mobilehomes.
- **Restricted affordability:** `affordable_housing_restricted` describes the statutory deed, regulatory, recorded, or qualifying subsidy restriction. A voucher or generic `is_subsidized` flag is not that determination.
- **Other exemptions:** `special_housing_exempt=false` is only an attestation that the relevant nonstandard statutory exceptions have been reviewed and found absent. It is not inferred from unit count or generic owner occupancy. Applicable exemptions differ by statute; category-specific exemption evidence remains necessary. A general positive flag cannot safely classify a tenancy under every different statute.
- **Separately alienable:** exemption requires separately alienable title, prescribed notice, and qualifying ownership. Corporations, REITs, and corporate-member LLCs do not qualify. A generic LLC flag does not prove all-natural-person membership or the absence of corporate members.
- **Owner/shared facilities:** the just-cause rule separately checks shared kitchen/bath with an owner. Owner-occupied single-family, duplex, ADU, tenancy-start, and continuous-occupancy exceptions require actual review; they are not inferred from `owner_occupied` alone.
- **Household clock:** `tenancy_months` represents at least one tenant’s continuous lawful occupation. Use `all_tenants_12_months` for the household proviso; one 18-month occupant is insufficient to resolve additional adult tenants. One tenant with 24 months is a separate decisive branch.
- **Small-owner deposit:** use the owner’s total properties/units, ownership form, and LLC membership, not this building’s unit count. The model does not emit an unconditional two-month limit because service-member status and all-natural-person LLC membership are not fully modeled.
- **Local flags:** `city_rent_controlled` and `city_eviction_covered` are distinct. Rent-exempt units can retain just cause. For LA, the eviction flag confirms the relevant RSO or JCO tenancy scope, including JCO six-month or original-lease-expiry activation and exceptions. Berkeley’s full/partial ordinance classification supports its deposit-interest flag use. Berkeley Fair Chance uses separate `city_fair_chance_covered` review.
- **Conditional events:** a relevant rule can apply to a property while a particular payment, fee, advertisement, increase, or eviction duty is triggered only by an actual event. The requirement explicitly states that event. No record asserts an existing event, violation, benefit amount, or software use from a parcel alone.

Known stricter local rent and just-cause coverage excludes the corresponding state coverage through the reviewed flags. Explicit `supersedes` relationships are also recorded. Unknown local coverage never silently becomes a definitive local replacement. Algorithm prohibitions are concurrent conduct standards; the bundle does not invent preemption between state antitrust law and local bans.

## Deliberate gaps and source conflicts

1. **Historical versions:** a current amended-section footer is not the first effective date of every unchanged provision. Null dates preserve historical uncertainty. The supplied April 2024 state rent-cap and March 2024 San Diego just-cause versions are identified as versions; missing earlier versions do not establish that there was no earlier protection.
2. **Berkeley activation:** D001 alone is a passed-to-print ordinance. S003 supplies final adoption, but not a precise effective date. Earlier proposals with suspended March 2026 language cannot supply the amended ordinance’s final activation date. No January/March date is guessed.
3. **LA source freshness:** D042’s 3% calculator period July 2025–June 2026 was expired at the challenge date. S001 supplies the independently dated July 2026–June 2027 period. D040 also contains older state CPI examples; those percentages are not imported as the current state cap.
4. **San Diego draft versus final:** D076 is an unsigned proposed ordinance. S002 final text adds the two-landlord condition and appraisal exception and permits attorney fees for a prevailing landlord or tenant. The draft is not substituted for current law.
5. **Algorithm proposals:** LA D039 is a motion/report request, not an enacted local rent-algorithm ban. No LA-specific ban is fabricated; statewide AB 325 remains relevant.
6. **Screening/interest gaps:** SF D078 redirects to a generic agency page without the operative Fair Chance scope. SF D083 publishes 4.2% deposit interest for 2026–27 but does not capture the linked eligibility and interest-duty rules. No unsupported property-wide SF screening or interest verdict is manufactured. Berkeley’s linked interest rate is likewise not guessed.
7. **Fees:** statewide §1950.6 retains the indexed $30 base formula. D005 guidance gives a 2026 amount, but the pack flags annual-cap verification ambiguity. This bundle does not elevate a number to a universally verified statewide indexed maximum without the underlying annual calculation.
8. **Relocation and notice-date modeling:** published schedules are preserved with conditions, not payment calculators. Rates depend on the event’s notice date and qualification. The current engine uses the research date for the annual record interval; users must separately review notice date. A future schedule boundary excludes the old record without proving there is no future duty. `repealed_on` is the existing interval-ending mechanism for annual records and does not mean the underlying ordinance itself was repealed.
